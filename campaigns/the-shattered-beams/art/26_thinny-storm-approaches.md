A vast wall of shimmering iridescent wrongness on the eastern horizon, 
stretching from north to south, advancing slowly across the Ashlands 
toward the viewer. The Thinny storm: where it touches the landscape, 
reality unravels -- trees at the storm's edge twist into shapes that have 
no name, the ground folds upward like pages in a burning book, light 
bends into spectra that shouldn't exist. The storm is not weather -- it is 
a wound in the world, iridescent and terrible, colors that the eye 
recognizes as wrong the way the tongue recognizes poison. In the middle 
distance, the flickering silhouette of a settlement (Loom) sits directly 
in the storm's path, its phasing structures visible against the advancing 
wall of unmaking. The foreground is Ashlands: cracked grey earth, dead 
trees, a road. The sky above the storm is cracked open, showing void -- 
not darkness, but an absence of everything. The scale is cosmic -- the 
storm dwarfs the landscape, dwarfs the settlement, dwarfs everything 
human. Wide panoramic shot, terrifying, sublime. Dark fantasy, painterly, 
atmospheric, apocalyptic.
