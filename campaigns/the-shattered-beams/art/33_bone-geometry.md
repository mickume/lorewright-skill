A wide expanse of ash-grey cracked earth in the Ashlands, dotted with 
small animal skeletons arranged in perfect geometric patterns. In the 
foreground, a fox skeleton forms a flawless hexagon from its vertebrae, 
ribs radiating outward in symmetrical lines, the skull at the exact 
center facing upward. Behind it, a bird skeleton spirals into a 
logarithmic curve. Further back, concentric circles of rodent bones, 
a jawbone bisecting the horizon at a precise angle. The patterns extend 
into the distance in every direction -- dozens of arrangements, each 
different, each mathematically perfect, each made from the bleached-white 
remains of creatures that died naturally and were rearranged by invisible 
forces. The sky is heavy amber-grey. Dead bone-white trees stand like 
sentinels. Crystalline fragments in the earth catch the sick light. The 
scene is beautiful and deeply wrong -- nature organized by alien 
intelligence into something too precise to be decorative, too deliberate 
to be coincidence. Low angle, wide shot. Dark fantasy exterior, painterly, 
atmospheric, unsettling.
