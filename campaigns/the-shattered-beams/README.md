# The Shattered Beams - Player Briefing

**Welcome to The Shattered Beams!**

This document is for players. It contains everything you need to know to create characters and prepare for Session 1. **Do not read** any other campaign documents — they contain spoilers!

---

## Campaign Overview

The world is broken. Two centuries ago, something called the Fracturing tore the boundaries between planes apart. Reality itself is held together by ancient forces that most people can't see and don't understand — forces that are failing. The sky is the wrong color. Time doesn't flow the way it should. Cities phase in and out of existence. And somewhere out there, someone is making it worse on purpose.

You don't know any of this yet. What you know is this: at a critical moment in your life, a door of impossible light appeared. You stepped through — or were pulled through — and woke up somewhere you've never been, surrounded by strangers who had the same experience.

You are bound together by something you don't understand. You can feel each other — a pull, a warmth, an awareness. And you can see things that others can't: the fractures in the world, the places where reality is thin, the fading light of something ancient that was never supposed to go dark.

This is a campaign about finding your place in a world that's coming apart, and deciding what — and who — is worth saving.

## Essential Information

**Campaign Type:** Story-driven with exploration and player choices that shape the narrative

**Starting Level:** 4

**Expected Length:** 7 sessions (3-4 hours each)

**Tone & Themes:**
- **Dark fantasy with wonder:** The world is dying, but it's beautiful in its decay. Ancient ruins catch starlight. Broken machines hum with residual magic. There is awe alongside the dread.
- **Found family:** Strangers bound together by forces beyond their understanding, learning to trust each other.
- **Moral complexity:** There are no easy answers. The people trying to save the world disagree violently about how to do it. Both sides have a point.
- **Post-apocalyptic survival:** The world has moved on. Communities survive through pragmatism and hard choices. Resources are scarce. Hope is scarcer.

## Setting & World

### The World After the Fracturing

Two hundred years ago, the boundaries between planes shattered in an event called the Fracturing. No one alive remembers what caused it. What everyone knows is what it left behind: a world where reality's rules are unreliable.

Most people have adapted. They know which roads are safe, which regions to avoid, and which times of day the sky is most likely to do something wrong. Settlements survive in pockets of relative stability, trading with each other when the paths between them cooperate.

### What Your Character Knows

- **The world is broken.** Everyone knows this. The sky is sometimes the wrong color. Time flows strangely in some places. Entire regions are uninhabitable.
- **Magic is unreliable.** Spells work, but ambient magical effects are unpredictable. Magitech (magical technology from a lost civilization) is scattered across the landscape — mostly non-functional, occasionally dangerous, rarely useful.
- **Ancient ruins exist.** The Old Ones — whoever they were — left behind structures of crystal and dark metal. Most people avoid them. Some people explore them. Few come back unchanged.
- **Reality storms happen.** Called "Thinnies," these tears in the fabric of reality bring wrongness: impossible geometry, creatures from elsewhere, and the dissolution of natural law. When a Thinny approaches, you run.

### Common Knowledge

**Currency:** Standard gold/silver/copper, supplemented by barter and magitech components (crystalline fragments are valuable trade goods).
**Languages:** Common is universal. Older languages appear in ruins but are largely untranslated.
**Magic:** Works normally for spellcasters, but the ambient magical field is erratic. Wild magic surges are more common than in a stable world.
**Religion:** The gods are distant. The Divine Gate — whatever remains of it — prevents direct divine intervention. Faith persists, but miracles are rare.

## Character Concept Guidance

### The Drawing

Your character was pulled through a "Door" — a rectangle of shimmering, impossible light — at a pivotal moment in their life. The Drawing was involuntary. You didn't choose this. The Door appeared at a moment of extreme emotion, crisis, or transformation, and you were pulled through before you could react.

**When building your character, decide:**
1. **Where were you?** What was your life like before the Drawing?
2. **What was happening?** What critical moment was interrupted? A battle? A betrayal? A loss? A discovery?
3. **What did you leave behind?** Who or what was on the other side of that Door? This is the thing your character carries.

### What Works Well

- **Characters with unfinished business.** The Drawing interrupted something. What?
- **Characters from different walks of life.** A soldier, a scholar, a thief, a healer — variety makes the party richer.
- **Characters who are skeptical.** You don't have to believe in cosmic destiny. You just have to deal with it.
- **Characters with strong personal motivations.** The cosmic stakes are revealed during play. Your character's personal stakes should exist from Session 1.

### Character Motivations

Your character should have a reason to:
1. **Keep going:** Why don't they just give up? What drives them forward?
2. **Work with strangers:** Why do they stay with people they just met? (The Constant provides a mechanical bond, but the emotional bond is yours to build.)
3. **Care about the world:** What in this broken world is worth fighting for?

Give your character:
- A **goal** (something they want to achieve or return to)
- A **fear** (something they're afraid of — about the world, or about themselves)
- A **secret** (something they're not ready to share with strangers)
- A **connection** (someone they left behind, or someone they're looking for)

### Party Cohesion

You don't know each other before Session 1. You'll meet in-game, bound together by a force you don't understand. The campaign is built around discovering *why* you were chosen and *what* you're supposed to do.

**During Session Zero**, we'll discuss your characters and make sure the group feels like people who could learn to trust each other — even if they start as reluctant allies.

## Session Zero Discussion Topics

### Content & Boundaries

**Content Warnings:** This campaign includes:
- Post-apocalyptic survival and scarcity
- Psychological horror (mind manipulation, forced captivity)
- Body horror (reality distortion affecting living things)
- Moral dilemmas with no clean answers
- Themes of imprisonment and exploitation
- Existential dread (the world itself is failing)

**Safety Tools:** We'll use Lines & Veils and an open-door policy. If something at the table is making you uncomfortable, say so — no explanation needed.

### Playstyle

This campaign balances:
- **Combat** (tactical encounters with environmental elements)
- **Roleplay** (NPC relationships, moral decisions, character moments)
- **Exploration** (ancient ruins, a broken landscape, mysteries to uncover)

Your choices matter. The story branches based on what you do, and not every outcome is good.

## Starting Scenario

### Where You'll Start

You wake up on a cold stone floor inside a place you've never been — a watchtower surrounded by a shimmering blue barrier. An old man is sitting by a fire, waiting. Outside the barrier, the world looks wrong.

### What's Happening

You've been Drawn — pulled through a Door from your previous life to this place. You don't know why. The old man says he does. Other strangers are waking up around you, just as confused.

### Why You're There

You don't know yet. That's the first mystery. But the old man says the world is dying, and something about you — about all of you — is the reason you're here.
