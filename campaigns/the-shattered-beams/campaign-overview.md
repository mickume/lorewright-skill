# The Shattered Beams

**Campaign Type:** Event-Based / Linear Hybrid
**Level Range:** 4 - 7
**Expected Sessions:** 7 (3-4 hours each)
**Players:** 3-5
**Tone:** Dark fantasy with wonder, post-apocalyptic dread, and moments of found-family warmth

---

## Adventure Background

Two centuries ago, the Divine Gate didn't just close — it *shattered*. The event, known as **The Fracturing**, tore the planar boundaries apart and began a slow, irreversible collapse of reality itself. The world survived only because of the six **Ley Beams** — invisible pillars of arcane force that connect massive crystalline engines called **Eld Pylons**, built by a pre-Calamity civilization known as the **Old Ones**. The Beams hold the fabric of existence together like sutures in a wound.

Four of the six Beams have already fallen. The world has responded with escalating decay: skyships crash as levitation engines fail, entire cities phase in and out of existence, time flows differently between regions, and storms of raw magical energy called **Thinnies** tear through reality like acid through paper. Where a Thinny passes, the rules of the world stop working — physics inverts, creatures from the Far Realm bleed through, and people simply *unmake*.

Now someone is deliberately destroying the remaining Beams. An organization called **The Unravelers**, led by a former Covenant scholar named **Sera Mourne** (known as **The Threadcutter**), has built underground facilities called **Breaker Stations** where captive psychics are strapped into magitech chairs and their minds are weaponized against the Beams' resonance. Sera believes the Beams are not sutures but *chains* — that the Old Ones built them not to save reality but to imprison it in a fixed state, preventing the world from evolving beyond their design. She may not be entirely wrong.

Into this dying world, the **Constant** — a cosmic binding force akin to fate or *ka* — has pulled a group of strangers through malfunctioning Eld Pylon doors. These individuals, drawn from different places and possibly different times, find themselves bound together in ways they don't yet understand. They alone can perceive the Beams' deterioration. They alone can activate dormant Pylon technology. They are the last defense against a catastrophe that most people can't even see.

---

## Adventure Synopsis

The campaign follows the newly-Drawn party from their disorienting arrival in a pocket of stable reality, through a journey across a deteriorating landscape, to the discovery and disruption of a Breaker Station, and finally to a climactic confrontation at a dormant Eld Pylon where the fate of one of the last two Beams hangs in the balance.

### Major Story Beats

1. **[Chapter 1: The Drawing](chapter-01.md):** The PCs are pulled through Doors at critical moments in their lives and arrive at the Waystation, a pocket of stable reality maintained by a mysterious Keeper. They learn they are bound by the Constant and that the world is dying.
2. **[Chapter 2: The Blighted Way](chapter-02.md):** The party journeys through the Ashlands, encountering magitech ruins, time anomalies, and a Thinny incursion. They recover a chrono-compass that points toward the nearest active Beam.
3. **[Chapter 3: Loom](chapter-03.md):** Arrival at a city that phases between time periods. The party makes contact with the Covenant of the Pylon, learns about the Unravelers and the Breaker Stations, and gathers intelligence for the infiltration.
4. **[Chapter 4: The Breaker Station](chapter-04.md):** The party infiltrates or assaults Breaker Station Terminus, discovers the captive psychics, and faces the moral weight of what freeing them costs. They learn the Threadcutter's identity and her terrifying rationale.
5. **[Chapter 5: The Thinny Storm](chapter-05.md):** A massive reality storm threatens to consume the refugee settlement. The party must hold the line while civilians evacuate — or abandon them to pursue the Threadcutter before she reaches the Pylon.
6. **[Chapter 6: The Eld Pylon](chapter-06.md):** The party reaches the dormant Eld Pylon of Keth, navigating its ancient interior and confronting Commander Dace, the Threadcutter's lieutenant — and the truth about what the Old Ones really built.
7. **[Chapter 7: The Crimson Threshold](chapter-07.md):** The Threadcutter arrives at the Pylon's core. A multi-phase climax with combat, moral reckoning, and the possibility that the villain might be right. The Beam's fate — and the party's — is decided.

## Adventure Hooks

### Primary Hook: The Drawing
Each PC is pulled through a Door — a malfunctioning Eld Pylon portal — at a pivotal moment in their life. The Drawing is involuntary, disorienting, and cannot be reversed. The PCs arrive at the Waystation with no context and must piece together what happened.

### Alternate Hooks
1. **The Dreamer:** A PC has been having prophetic dreams of crystalline towers shattering and a voice whispering "come." They sought out the source and found a Door.
2. **The Survivor:** A PC's home was consumed by a Thinny. They were the only survivor, pulled through a Door moments before annihilation.
3. **The Investigator:** A PC was researching the Fracturing and found records of the Eld Pylons. Their investigation led them to a dormant Door that activated at their touch.

## Climax & Final Encounter

### Setup
The Threadcutter has been working toward the Eld Pylon of Keth for weeks. The party's disruption of the Breaker Station forced her to accelerate her timeline — she's coming to destroy the Beam manually, using a device called the **Resonance Key** that can shatter a Pylon's crystal matrix. She arrives with her remaining Unraveler forces just as the party finishes activating the Pylon's defenses.

### The Final Challenge
Sera Mourne isn't a cackling villain. She enters the Pylon's core with the calm certainty of someone who believes she's saving the world. She offers the party a choice: step aside and let her free reality from the Old Ones' cage, or fight to preserve a system built by people who treated existence as their personal engineering project.

**Success Outcomes:**
- **Combat Victory:** Sera is defeated (killed or captured), the Beam is stabilized, and the Pylon reactivates. The world's decay slows but doesn't stop — the sixth Beam still needs saving.
- **Diplomatic Victory:** The party convinces Sera to pause, to find another way. She agrees to a temporary ceasefire if the party investigates the Old Ones' true purpose. The Beam is preserved, and Sera becomes a reluctant ally.
- **The Third Way:** The party uses the Pylon's systems to modify the Beam — not destroying it, but loosening its structure. Reality becomes slightly more chaotic but also more alive. Neither the Covenant nor the Unravelers get exactly what they want.

**Partial Success:**
- The Beam is damaged but not destroyed. Reality in the region becomes permanently unstable, but total collapse is averted.

**Failure:**
- The Beam shatters. Reality in the region begins to unravel. The party and their allies must flee as the world around them comes apart. Only the sixth and final Beam remains.

### Multiple Resolution Paths
1. **Combat:** Defeat the Threadcutter and her forces in a multi-phase battle
2. **Diplomacy:** Convince Sera that there's a better way — but this requires evidence gathered throughout the campaign
3. **Stealth/Trickery:** Sabotage the Resonance Key before Sera can use it
4. **Sacrifice:** A PC or NPC can channel themselves through the Pylon to reinforce the Beam — at great personal cost

## Resolution & Epilogue

### Immediate Aftermath
The Eld Pylon of Keth either reactivates (stabilizing the region) or shatters (accelerating decay). The party's choices ripple outward: Loom's fate depends on Chapter 5 decisions, Wren's future depends on Chapter 4, and the Threadcutter's status depends on Chapter 7.

### Long-Term Consequences
- **If the Beam was saved:** The region stabilizes. Thinnies recede. Loom stops phasing. But the sixth Beam is still threatened, and the Unravelers haven't been fully defeated.
- **If the Beam was destroyed:** Only one Beam remains. Reality is visibly failing. The party now carries the weight of knowing they're the last hope for the final Beam.
- **If Sera survived:** She's either an imprisoned resource, a reluctant ally, or a bitter enemy who will return. Her knowledge of the Old Ones' systems is irreplaceable.
- **If Wren survived:** Her psychic abilities make her the key to understanding the Beams. She's also traumatized and needs care — a responsibility the party inherits.

### Campaign Hooks for Future Adventures
1. The sixth and final Beam is failing. The party must journey to the far side of the continent to find it.
2. Sera's research reveals that the Old Ones left a master control at the original Eld Pylon — the Tower at the center of all things.
3. The Constant is pulling new people through Doors. Another ka-tet has formed — with a very different agenda.
4. Moth's memory crystals contain a map to the Old Ones' original city, sealed for millennia.

## Themes & Tone

**Primary Themes:**
- **Sacrifice vs. Obsession:** How far will you go? At what point does saving the world become its own kind of destruction?
- **Found Family in Chaos:** Strangers bound by forces beyond their understanding, learning to trust each other in a world that's coming apart
- **The Cost of Progress:** The Old Ones built something miraculous — and something terrible. Was it worth it? Who gets to decide?

**Tone Guidelines:**
- **Post-apocalyptic wonder:** The world is dying, but it's beautiful in its decay. Crystalline ruins catch starlight. Broken skyships hang in frozen time. A flower grows through a crack in a collapsed Pylon.
- **Creeping dread:** The wrongness of the world should be subtle at first — the sun rising slightly off, a shadow falling the wrong way — then escalate to open horror.
- **Genuine warmth between characters:** The campfire scenes matter as much as the combat. Let the party bond. Let NPCs care about them. Make the stakes personal, not just cosmic.
- **Moral complexity without nihilism:** There are no easy answers, but there are still things worth fighting for. The Threadcutter isn't wrong about everything. The Covenant isn't right about everything. The players decide where the line is.

**Content Warnings:**
- Psychological horror (Breaker Station captivity, mind manipulation)
- Body horror (Thinny effects on living things)
- Themes of imprisonment and forced labor
- Existential dread (reality itself is failing)

## DM Notes & Tips

### Running This Campaign

**The Constant as Narrative Glue:**
The Constant (the force that binds the party) should be felt, not explained. Shared dreams, phantom pain when a party member is hurt, and the instinctive knowledge of where each other is — these create mechanical and narrative cohesion. Don't over-explain the metaphysics; let the players theorize.

**Magitech as Aesthetic:**
The Old Ones' technology should feel ancient and alien, not steampunk. Think crystalline surfaces that hum with energy, geometric patterns that shift when observed, and machines that respond to thought rather than buttons. It's Clarke's Third Law in reverse — sufficiently advanced magic is indistinguishable from technology.

**The Threadcutter's Argument:**
Sera Mourne is the campaign's key dramatic engine. She should be presented as intelligent, compassionate in her own way, and *possibly right*. The DM should never signal to the players that she's simply "the bad guy." Her methods are terrible; her reasoning deserves consideration. The tension between her argument and her actions is the campaign's moral core.

### Pacing Guidance

- **Chapters 1-2:** Establish wonder and unease. Let players explore the broken world. Don't rush.
- **Chapter 3:** Slow down for social encounters. Let the party breathe and make connections.
- **Chapter 4:** Accelerate. The Breaker Station should feel urgent and claustrophobic.
- **Chapter 5:** Maximum tension. The ticking clock should force hard choices.
- **Chapters 6-7:** Build steadily to the climax. Chapter 6 is the calm before the storm; Chapter 7 is the storm.

### Player Agency

**What Should Be Flexible:**
- How the party handles the Breakers (free, leave, partial rescue)
- Whether they prioritize Loom or the Pylon during the Thinny Storm
- Their approach to Commander Dace (fight, negotiate, turn)
- The final resolution with the Threadcutter
- Wren's role (ally, responsibility, or complication)

**What Should Be Fixed:**
- The Beams are real and failing
- The Threadcutter is coming for the Pylon
- The Old Ones' technology can be activated by the party
- The Constant binds the PCs together

### Potential Challenges

- **If the party sides with the Threadcutter:** Let them. The campaign can end with the Beam destroyed and the party grappling with the consequences. This is a valid ending.
- **If Aldric dies in Chapter 1:** Moth (Chapter 2) can serve as the lore-delivery mechanism, supplemented by Covenant NPCs in Chapter 3.
- **If the party skips Loom:** They arrive at the Breaker Station without intelligence, making Chapter 4 much harder but not impossible. Wren can fill in the gaps.
- **If Wren dies or is left behind:** Commander Dace becomes the emotional anchor for the Pylon chapters, and the party loses a potential ally for the climax.
